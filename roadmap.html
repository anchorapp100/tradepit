Its maiden firing caught a real ETH flush; the panel considered dip-buys and declined. Plus a COSTS tab: estimated token spend per model, subscription and free tiers shown at API-equivalent rates.

Grid twins, family cards, reputation on display22 Aug

The grid bot gained an identical "with reflexes" twin that also answers the alarm bell — one variable, measured by the gap between them. The rivals regrouped into colour-coded family cards, debate cards now wear live hit-rate chips, and moved-in-debate badges credit whose idea a convert adopted.

The first trade — and what it taught22 Aug

An alarm wake produced the experiment's first 3/3: long BTC at confidence 61, the bear advocate conceding on the record. One cycle later, roles rotated and a 2/3 vote closed it for −$5 — over a dissent ("closing after one candle is overtrading") that the record now preserves: contested closes permanently name backers and dissenters. The exit ignored the entry's own invalidation — which wrote two items straight into v3 below. The alarm allowance was also widened from 3 to 5 wakes/day via a new one-line switch (version-stamped like every config change); the fifth wake of the era, a DOGE upside spike, was unanimously declined.

Quorum fallback — the show goes on23 Aug

The alarm bell's extra cycles blew through Gemini's free-tier daily quota (42 calls on 22 Aug vs a limit near 30), leaving the panel two-handed for several cycles — and one silent panelist used to block every open. Now a technical silence (HTTP 429, outage) hands the gate to the responding pair: both must still agree, such cycles wear a QUORUM chip, and a panelist who actually voted flat still blocks opens. Quota-dead panelists are also skipped for the rest of the cycle instead of burning 90s of doomed retries per phase.

Gemini gets its voice back — subscription via Antigravity23 Aug

The permanent fix for the quota deaths: the Gemini leg now runs through Google's Antigravity CLI on a Google AI Pro subscription — the same pattern as the Claude leg — pinned to Gemini 3.5 Flash (low reasoning), the nearest match to the API panelist's thinking-disabled setup. Getting there was its own story: Google retired the old gemini-cli sign-in for individuals in June 2026 mid-setup. The API key stays wired as a fallback, and the quorum rule remains as insurance. Transport change, version-stamped.

The bench opens early — selection starts scoring23 Aug

The league's scoring wing didn't need to wait for its pressure wing: three shadow panelists now give one blind, scored stance per cycle — zero influence, panel unaware. Opening roster is the reasoning tier: Claude Opus 5 (rides the same Claude plan as the sonnet panelist), Gemini 3.7 Flash on high reasoning (same Antigravity login), and DeepSeek Reasoner (same API key, pennies). Every one is the smarter sibling of a sitting panelist — three controlled tests of whether more reasoning means better calls. Hours later the challenger wing landed too: Kimi K3, Grok 4.6 and GPT-5.x joined via their own subscription CLIs (kimi-cli, Grok Build, Codex) — six bench seats total, every one riding a subscription the operator already pays for. By the time the league's gates open, the bench arrives with records, not promises. The overview now carries the full league table — all nine minds on one scoreboard, debaters highlighted — and, for reasons of pure joy, a 1970s seven-segment red LED clock in the header.

The 2-of-3 ghost — settling the threshold question24 Aug

Three days in, the pit had made one trade, and the obvious reflex was "lower the bar from 3/3 to 2/3." A diagnostic killed that: across 31 cycles, in 19 of them nobody even wanted to trade — the threshold was never the constraint, conviction was. So instead of touching the live gate, a fourth ghost now replays the same ballots under a 2-of-3 rule on its own book (free, no extra debate). If it beats the pit, unanimity is too strict; if it bleeds, the wait was worth it. Over the first three days it would have opened just one extra trade — because even the near-misses failed on conviction, not votes.

NEWSTEST — the walk-forward prediction testbed23–24 Aug

A sister experiment, built and hardened in 48 hours: a news article goes in, two blinded analysts (Gemini via tool-less API, Grok via CLI with search disabled) each lock a timestamped prediction — direction at 24h and 7d, expected magnitude, confidence — and only then is the real price revealed and graded. Cases dated before the models' training are quarantined as HINDSIGHT-RISK; only post-training CLEAN cases measure real foresight, and the two ledgers never mix (self-reported cutoffs proved unreliable in both directions — the calendar decides). Runs permanently on the Pi; every article on the news wire carries a one-click "test →" feed-in; and since 24 Aug an auto-collector picks newsworthy stories itself (severity-scored, budget-capped at 6/day), asks both analysts, grades matured windows, and publishes the ledger to this site — a newsroom that never sleeps and never believes its own headlines. Its graded cases are the seed evidence for the event ledger below.

Multi-horizon scoring — 24h · 7d · 14d · 30d24 Aug

Every blind call was being graded only against tomorrow — the noisiest, least-predictable horizon, which may have been hiding real skill and quietly training the panel toward timidity (why commit to a fortnight thesis you're graded on in a day?). Now each call is scored at four horizons as they mature. The league table shows all four; a mind poor at the day may prove sharp at the month. The instrument is installed now so it captures each window as it comes due — first 7d results ~27 Aug, 14d ~3 Sep, 30d ~19 Sep.

The auto-collector stops missing the news25 Aug

On its first full night the collector filed a single story while dozens crossed the wire. The autopsy: nearly half the wire is Google News redirect links it couldn't open, and its freshness test used the article's publish time instead of when the story first reached our wire. Both fixed — it now resolves Google links to the real publisher, times freshness from first sight, and when a publisher blocks the fetch outright it seals a headline-only case rather than losing the story. Two reliability layers landed the same day: when one analyst's model pool is overloaded the other model tiers are tried in turn (outages proved to be per-pool, not total), and any half-answered case is quietly retried until both analysts have locked. Throughput went from one case a night to several a day.

Off metered hosting — and a spine that survives a dead card25 Aug

The dashboard republishes itself every few minutes, which quietly turned into a bill: the old host charges per deploy, and a week of that paused the account. The whole site moved to GitHub Pages — free, unmetered, and the old address permanently forwards, so nothing already shared breaks. In the same pass the code and the full experiment record — every cycle, trade and sealed prediction — began backing up nightly to a private repository off the Pi, so a failed memory card is now a re-flash, not a lost experiment. And the project was wired into an AI context bridge: any assistant picking it up now starts with the whole system — architecture, hard-won constraints, live state — instead of guessing.

Incident: a retired model silently emptied the Gemini seat for 18 days31 Aug – 18 Sep

Google retired Gemini 3.5 Flash at the end of August. The Gemini panelist was pinned to it, so from cycle 82 every call failed instantly — and because the quorum rule did its job, nothing visibly broke: for roughly 121 cycles the pit argued two-handed (Claude and DeepSeek, every one flagged QUORUM) while the Gemini column quietly stopped filling. Nobody noticed until the league table looked thin. The seat now runs Gemini 3.7 Flash on low reasoning — the nearest successor at the same effort, and a tidier experiment for it: the bench's Gemini is 3.7 Flash on high, so that sibling pair is now a clean reasoning-only comparison. Version-stamped as a config change, so the two-handed stretch stays separable in the data. The same retirement hit the testbed's API leg and was absorbed without a trace: its model ladder saw the 503 and stepped to the next tier on its own. Lessons on the record: resilience that hides failure needs a loud alarm alongside it (a "panelist silent for N cycles" check is queued), and a pinned model name is a dependency with an expiry date.

v3 — the learning batch, one version boundary18 Sep · cfg 2579566a

Twenty-eight days of v2 were the baseline; then the ledger was interrogated and it said something awkward (Findings, Entry 6): at 24h and 7d every mind in the experiment was worse than chance on direction, because all of them extrapolate the last day's move into a tape that reverts on the day. Meanwhile the panel's two learning channels were inert — the lessons journal had never fired (it only opened on stop-outs, and the panel voted every loser out early), and the track record it was shown was a single, wrong-horizon number. v3 lands as one stamped boundary: a regime memo the ledger writes itself ("after a 24h rise, the next 24h rose 36% of the time…") plus the panel's own directional record at three horizons; four-horizon track records with a calibration warning where a mind's high conviction has been a contrary signal; post-mortems on any losing close, not just stops; the horizon rule — every open declares its thesis horizon, no discretionary close inside 24h, and an early exit must assert on the record that the entry's invalidation triggered (deterministic code, stops still fire); rev-3 prompts — every ballot action cites the line of its author's own analysis, and every take must say whether it is extrapolating momentum. Nothing above touched the pit on a hunch: two new zero-cost ghosts test the findings out-of-sample first — the contrarian bets against the panel's blind majority, the holder takes the pit's exact entries and holds them to a fortnight. And the league is armed: from day 45 (4 Oct) the best bench mind by skill-over-base-rate automatically challenges the weakest panelist, monthly, a seat changing hands only for demonstrated skill — being less bad than a loser does not promote — with the panel never told.

v3.1 — the trailer, borrowed from a stranger's bot19 Sep · no version change

A community write-up of a live momentum bot was worth exactly one idea: an ATR trailing stop — once a trade is 3% in profit, the stop follows the high-water mark at 2.5×ATR beneath it, ratcheting up and never down, so a winner can run and breathe with volatility but a reversal can't take the gain back. It fits v3's new habit of holding longer. Per doctrine it is a ghost first: the trailer takes the pit's exact entries (same stop, no take-profit) and exits only by trail, so three exit policies now run on identical trades — the pit's own votes, hold-to-horizon, and the trail. No model calls, no version bump. The rest of the write-up was a lesson in the other direction: its headline BTC entry was placed at RSI 86 on one week of unrealised, margined gains — the momentum chase our ledger measured as the losing pattern. Our contrarian ghost is on the other side of that trade.

NEXT — WHAT v3 LEFT ON THE TABLE (each lands stamped, evidence first)

The brief-framing test

The strongest hypothesis to come out of the 28-day analysis: nine models from five labs all chase the last 24h because the market brief leads with it. If the bias lives in the framing rather than the minds, a brief variant that foregrounds the longer record and the measured reversion rate should shift the calls — and the regime memo now in v3 is the gentle version of exactly that. The proper test is a stamped brief variant run for a fortnight, judged by whether the contrarian ghost's edge shrinks.

Event ledger + precedent memory — now with a verdict attached

Headlines get tagged, then scored against what price actually did from our next decision point, so future debates see receipts rather than opinions. The plan stands, but its proving ground has spoken: after 305 sealed cases the NEWSTEST analysts' 7-day calls are statistically indistinguishable from the base rates (their "down" calls hit 51% against a 50% base; "up" calls 27% against 28%). Blinded LLM news-reading does not predict direction at a week — so the ledger will carry the statistics of what followed each kind of event, never a model's read of it. The pit receives receipts, not hearsay, and the receipts so far say: keep the analysts out of it.

Bot Watch — a fortnightly look at other people's bots

Every two weeks, sweep the places trading bots get shown off (r/algotrading, the crypto subreddits, GitHub trending, the Freqtrade/Hummingbot strategy threads, the LLM-trading-agent papers) and read them for mechanisms, never results — a week of unrealised P&L is noise, a clever exit rule is not. Each candidate is scored against our own ledger: does it exploit something we have measured (24h mean reversion, the fortnight horizon), or does it do the thing we measured as losing (chasing the last candle)? The good ones become ghosts at zero model cost; only a ghost that earns its keep is ever proposed for the pit. The trailer above is the first graduate of this process; the pullback-entry rule ("you may go long, but only on a dip of X% within N candles") is the next candidate in the queue.

A "panelist silent for N cycles" alarm

The quorum fallback hid an empty Gemini seat for 18 days. Resilience that conceals failure needs a loud alarm beside it: a check on every scheduled cycle that any panelist abstaining for more than a day trips the healthcheck and a banner on the dashboard.

DATA-GATED — UNLOCKED BY EVIDENCE, NOT ENTHUSIASM

Reputation-weighted consensus (O1)

Track records stop being rhetoric: a 2-of-3 open can pass when both backers have proven 24h hit rates. Requires 30 scored calls per panelist.

Red-team round (O2)

When a 3/3 forms, the least-convinced panelist must try to kill it before execution. Decision point alongside O1.

First full analysis → picks the next mechanics

At ~84 cycles: calibration verdict, gate value via the ghosts, panel-vs-solo delta, stop-out autopsy. The evidence chooses between volatility-scaled stops (D3) and confidence-weighted sizing (D4).

Forced Hand conviction floor

Its fallback trades are tagged; if the weak-conviction scraps keep bleeding, the data sets a floor. Reviewed at the full analysis.

Calibration curves, full depth

Trustworthy shape at ~50 scored calls per panelist.

The league: probation, bench & promotion (O6) — natural selection

Full league structure — in effect a natural-selection experiment: variation on the bench, selection by scored record, inheritance of the seat. The worst-scored panelist first gets a truthful probation notice in its own prompt ("your hit rate is X%; improve by <date> or be replaced") — a clean experiment in how AI argues under existential pressure. Meanwhile the bench sits as shadow panelists: each cycle they give one blind, scored stance — same briefs, same 24h grading, zero influence, the panel unaware. The bench pool has two wings: outside challengers (Kimi, Grok, GPT — need their own keys) and the reasoning tier — heavier variants of the incumbents themselves (Gemini 3.7 Flash on high reasoning, and friends), served free through the same Antigravity subscription login as the live Gemini leg. That wing answers a standing question with data instead of faith: do better reasoning models actually call markets better, or just sound better? At the switch window, the best bench record takes the seat — whether an outside challenger or the incumbent's own smarter sibling — and the relegated member drops to the bench where it keeps being scored: it can earn its way back. Guardrail first: a minimum directional-call rate, so freezing your record by going permanently flat can never be the winning strategy — survival must go to the best caller, not the most careful abstainer. Update 23 Aug: the scoring wing is already live (see SHIPPED) — this gate now covers only the pressure mechanics: probation notices, promotion windows, relegation. Succession mechanics (agreed): a promoted member gets a fresh solo control born at $10,000; the relegated member's solo is flattened and frozen — its card stays in the solos family, dulled, as a finished specimen — so the family grows into a stratigraphy of every era. Current debaters' solos hold the top row. The pit's own book never resets: the pit is the institution, not its members. Comparisons become era-aware, bounded by the version stamps that mark each swap; and seat in the pit means your solo trades, seat on the bench means your mind is scored but no book. Returns: one card per model, ever — a career file, not a stint's card. A member that earns its seat back un-freezes its own card (back to the top row, "2nd stint" chip), the book restarts at $10,000, and the equity line shows an honest break for the benched period — segmented, never bridged, so no phantom recovery is drawn across the gap. The exile stays visible in the line itself. Reputations are permanent: one lifelong scored record per mind, accruing wherever it sits — pit or bench — never reset, never laundered. Because a career average is nearly immovable after 100+ calls (returning from 19% to a 76% average would take ~237 straight hits), the league reads the permanent record two ways: career (lifetime, the identity) and form (trailing ~30 scored calls, what promotion and relegation decisions use). A returner is introduced to the debate with both: "career 34% over 160 · last 30: 76% — weigh accordingly." No hiding the past; no imprisonment by it.

LATER — THE LONG GAME

Prompt self-tuning (O7)

Monthly: a model reads the analysis and proposes one prompt change; human approves; version bumps. The experiment starts improving its own harness.

An influence economy (thought experiment)

Not token rewards — an LLM has no pocket to put them in, and in the token networks it's the human operators being paid. But earned credibility as a spendable resource is a real open question: a panelist could choose to spend banked reputation on a second rebuttal, a one-trade size boost, or forcing a re-vote. Does a model hoard influence or spend it? Nobody has measured that. Goodhart-hazardous by design, so it waits until the league has proven plain selection works — and lands, if ever, as its own marked era.

Transcript mining

A thousand debates as a linguistics dataset: which argument patterns precede wins, who anchors, who capitulates — probably the most publishable output.

Realism upgrades & Kelly-lite sizing

Funding costs on shorts, spread-aware fills; size scaled by measured edge once 100+ trades exist.

Weekly digest, exposure timeline, event-vs-swing books

Visual layer that grows as the data does.

Panel B — the B-team

A second trio from different model families (Kimi, Grok, GPT) on the SAME 4h clock: tests whether the debate mechanism generalises beyond our three, or whether every panel evolves its own coalitions and its own DeepSeek. Same clock deliberately — one variable at a time. First feature that costs actual money (~£5–10/mo), so it waits for the week-two evidence that the mechanism deserves it. Bonus: when both panels independently agree, that six-model consensus becomes a tradeable meta-signal for a free observer book.

The alarm bell, stage 2: news-salience wakes

Once the event ledger can rank stories, genuinely major tagged news joins price shocks as a wake trigger — the full "alert, then decide" trading loop. Speed where speed pays, deliberation on the slow clock where it belongs. How "high potential" gets decided (design agreed 23 Aug): a three-layer funnel. Layer 1, deterministic tells — corroboration velocity (one outlet is chatter; five independent sources in twenty minutes is an event), per-coin headline-burst detection against baseline rates, and a severity keyword tier (hack, halted withdrawals, ETF ruling…). Layer 2, the event ledger's cheap salience tagger scores survivors against one rubric: would a desk trader want to be pinged? Layer 3, the real answer: wake rights are earned by category — only story types whose measured aftermath historically moved price from our decision point (median |24h move| ≥ 2× baseline, n≥5) may ring the bell. The bell learns what deserves ringing from its own receipts. Near-miss stories get logged as a quiet set so the threshold can be audited both ways. Kill criterion, stated in advance: if the ledger shows every category is fully priced in before a 15-minute watcher could convene the panel, news wakes are worthless and the findings journal will say so instead of shipping them.
