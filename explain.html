TRADEPIT

what is this, in plain English

The idea in one minute

Three different AIs — Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek — are locked in a room (well, a Raspberry Pi) and asked to trade cryptocurrency together with pretend money. The twist: none of them can trade alone. To open a trade, all three have to argue it out and agree. If they can't agree, nothing happens — and doing nothing is allowed.

The question we're actually asking isn't "can AI get rich?" (it can't — and this is fake money anyway). It's: do three AIs that must convince each other make better decisions than one AI on its own? Does debate add wisdom, or just slow everything down?

What happens every 4 hours

1
The computer gathers fresh market data — prices, momentum, market mood, news headlines — for five big coins: Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP and Dogecoin.
2
Each AI writes down its honest opinion in secret, before seeing what the others think. No copying.
3
Then they debate. Each one is handed a role that rotates every round — one argues the optimistic case, one the pessimistic case, one plays the cautious risk manager — so somebody is always pushing back.
4
Everyone casts a final vote. A new trade needs 3 out of 3. Closing an existing trade needs 2 out of 3. No agreement, no trade. (If one AI's service is down or out of quota — silence, not an opinion — the vote falls to the two who answered, and both must agree. An AI that actually votes "do nothing" still counts as a no.)
5
Boring, reliable Python code — not the AIs — enforces the safety rules: small position sizes, mandatory stop-losses, an emergency brake if losses pile up. The AIs cannot override it.
6
Everything gets written down — every argument, vote and trade — and this website updates itself.

The alarm bell

Markets don't keep office hours, so neither does the panel. A watcher checks prices every 15 minutes, and if any coin suddenly moves more than 3%, it rings the bell and drags all three AIs into an emergency session — the way a human trader gets a price alert on their phone and then decides whether it matters. The briefing note says exactly why they were woken, plus one line of adult supervision: "an alert is information, not an instruction to trade." Wakes are rationed — currently up to five a day, at least two hours apart — so an alert stays a signal, not background noise. The solo AIs get woken too (fair race), and so does one special grid bot with reflexes; everything else sleeps through it on purpose.

The news experiment

Alongside the trading race runs a second question: can an AI read a news story and predict what it means for price — before the answer exists? A robot editor watches the crypto news wire around the clock and picks out the few stories that matter each day. Two AIs (from different companies, with their internet access switched off so they can't peek at the ending) each write down a sealed, timestamped prediction. Only later is the real price revealed and the prediction marked right or wrong — in public, at the prediction testbed. Early lesson worth knowing: when asked about the past, AIs sound like confident historians; asked the same question about the future, they hedge like the rest of us. Only the sealed-in-advance kind counts.

Who's racing

The debating trio (we call them The Pit) starts with $10,000 of pretend money. So does every rival they have to beat:

  • Each AI trading solo — same data, no debate. If the trio can't beat its own members, the debate is pointless.
  • Four rule-based bots — no AI at all, just old-school maths: a trend-follower running on two clocks (4-hourly and hourly — does trading faster help, or just burn fees?) and a buy-the-dip grid bot that loves choppy markets, plus an identical grid twin that also gets woken by the alarm bell — same rules, faster reflexes, and the gap between the twins measures whether reflexes help. This is what most "AI trading bot" products secretly are.
  • A coin flip — random trades. Pure luck, as a floor.
  • Just buying Bitcoin and holding it — the famously hard benchmark to beat.
  • The Forced Hand — a shadow account that must always trade the panel's single strongest idea, agreement or not. Comparing it to The Pit measures exactly what all that cautious arguing costs — or saves.
  • Three "ghost" accounts — one secretly trades the ideas the trio rejected, one trades what the safety code vetoed, and one replays the same votes but only needs 2 of 3 to agree instead of all 3 — so we can see whether demanding unanimity is too cautious. The AIs don't know the ghosts exist.

They keep score on themselves

Every secret opinion each AI writes down gets graded against what the market actually did — not just tomorrow, but at four timescales: 24 hours, 7 days, 14 days and 30 days, because a good trader reads more than one horizon and a model that's poor at tomorrow may be sharp on the fortnight. No exceptions, no excuses. The running scorecards are then read back to all three before every debate ("claude 80% · deepseek 17% · gemini 71% — weigh each other's arguments accordingly"), so a model that's been wrong a lot walks into the room with everyone knowing it. When a trade loses money, one of them has to write the post-mortem into a shared lessons journal that future debates re-read. Whether AIs actually learn from being scored is one of the experiment's big questions. And every record is permanent — a lifelong ledger per AI that survives promotions, relegations and comebacks. Nobody gets to start over.

The bench

Six more AIs watch from the shadows: smarter, slower versions of the three debaters (Claude Opus, a higher-reasoning Gemini, DeepSeek's "thinking" model) and three outside challengers from other labs (Kimi, Grok, GPT-5). Every cycle, each writes down its own secret market call and gets graded exactly like the debaters — but they have no influence, no money, and no idea they're competing. The debaters don't know the bench exists either. One day, when enough scores are in, the bench member with the best record will challenge for a real seat at the debate table — natural selection, with judges who never know they're being judged.

What to look at on the dashboard

  • Equity by arm — the race itself. Every line started at $10,000; up is winning.
  • Gate value — the headline number: the debating trio's return minus the Forced Hand's. Positive means all that arguing is earning money; negative means it's costing.
  • The Cycles tab — the debates themselves, word for word. Genuinely entertaining: they concede points, snipe at each other's logic, and occasionally talk one another out of bad ideas. Each speaker wears their live scorecard next to their name, and when a vote to close a trade is carried over an objection, the record names who objected.
  • The Analysis tab — the behaviour lab: which AI is overconfident, who changes their mind, who always agrees with whom, and whether being assigned "the optimist" role secretly biases a vote.
  • The News tab — a live crypto news feed, plus the exact headlines the AIs were shown before each debate.
  • The league table — all nine minds on one scoreboard, debaters highlighted, bench in the shadows, ranked by the same blind test. Watch the bottom six build records the top three know nothing about.

Why bother?

Committees of AIs are quietly becoming a real thing — models checking other models' work, voting on answers, debating decisions. Almost nobody measures whether that actually helps. Trading is a brutal and honest test bed: decisions are frequent, outcomes are numeric, and excuses don't move the price. Whatever we learn about how these models argue, concede, bluff and miscalibrate is the real prize — the fake money is just the scoreboard.

The important small print

No real money is involved anywhere — it's a simulation with play-money accounts. Nothing on this site is financial advice, and the AIs are frequently, confidently wrong (measuring exactly how wrong is half the point). Results over days or weeks are mostly luck; we say so on the dashboard wherever the sample is small. The experiment changes over time as we add instruments — every change is version-stamped in the data so before and after stay honest.
